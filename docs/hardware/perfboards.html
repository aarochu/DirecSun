Changed from the CAD plan

  • Cut the carrier from a 5 × 7 cm board: keep one original 50 mm edge (the USB-C end, row A) and score between rows L and M, about 35 mm down. Dry-fit it in the avionics deck's guides before soldering.
  • The battery divider sits on the power deck, not the carrier, so no 10 V wire reaches the XIAO. Only the divided tap (about 1.8 V) goes up the cable.
  • One 4-wire cable joins the decks: battery tap, GND, 5 V and the ESC signal. It is soldered to the power deck and plugs onto J3 on the carrier, so the carrier needs only your 7 header pins.
  • Move the two divider resistors from the breadboard as they are. The firmware is calibrated to their ratio (1.761 V at 10.01 V). If you use different ones, measure the tap against a known supply and send me both numbers.

Needed for the demo

  • Carrier: XIAO, Hall header and pull-up, ESC header and series resistor, I²C header, and the VBAT and GND pins to the power deck.
  • Power deck: the MPM3610 (already in), the input pads with the inline fuse, both buses, C1, the ESC pads, the divider, and the 4-wire cable to the carrier.
  • Optional, dashed on the board: the sun sensor parts (J5, J6, R3, R4).

Which wire, and how to lay a bus

  • The two power buses (power deck rows M and S): your 18 AWG stranded, stripped bare, strands twisted tight and tinned along the whole length so it turns into a stiff bar. Jumpers: the 22 AWG solid with its insulation on, cut and stripped as the Jumpers table says. Everything else is solder traces.
  • Solder the parts first and clip their leads to about 2 mm on the solder side.
  • Put a little solder on every pad that has a dot on the bus. Lay the tinned bar flat along the row, against the clipped leads, and hold it with tape or a clip.
  • Reflow the two end dots first, then each dot in between, feeding a little solder until it wraps the bar, the pad, and the lead together.
  • Lap the thick ESC and battery leads onto the bar at their pads: tin the lead, lay it along the bar, and heat both.

How to read the drawing

Jumper (component side). Insulated 22 AWG solid lying on top. Its two stripped ends go down through the square holes; the Jumpers table gives the route and the cut length.
Solder trace (solder side). Join every pad inside the silver outline with solder, one pad to the next, and touch nothing outside it. A trace of two pads is a single bridge.
Bus (solder side, power deck only). Bare tinned 18 AWG laid along the holes, soldered at each dot.
Wire leaving the board. Goes into that hole from the component side and is soldered there. The Wires leaving the board table says where it goes and how long to cut it.
  • Standing resistors: the body sits on the hole with the circle, and the lead bends over to the other hole.
  • The solder side view is mirrored left to right, exactly as the board looks when you turn it over. Hole names stay the same.
  • Hole names count column 1 from the left and row A from the top, component side up, as drawn. If your board's printed numbers run the other way, go by the drawing, not the print.

Cables between the decks

Every cable unplugs at the carrier, so the stack can still come apart. Thread the Hall cable and the J7 cable up through each deck's wire slot as you slide the decks down the rods (motor, power, battery, avionics, top), and plug them into the carrier last. Signal cables use the −Y slots, next to the carrier's headers. Motor phases and supply leads use the +Y slots and stay twisted, away from the signals and from the magnetometer on the top deck (TD §5.5). Battery box wiring

From cad/README.md. Eight AA cells, two rows of four, alternating direction: follow the + and − marks on the holder's top face for the top row; in every column the bottom cell points the other way. Each contact is an M3 screw; outside the cap, stack a washer, the wire loop, a washer, and the nut. Leave slack in every bridge wire so it does not hold the spring. Both caps are drawn as you see them from outside.

  1. Cap B: four bridge wires, each joining the top and bottom contact of one column.
  2. Cap A: three bridge wires: top row columns 1 to 2, bottom row columns 2 to 3, top row columns 3 to 4. Columns count from the −Y side of the box (cap A faces −X), so from outside cap A column 1 is on your right.
  3. Leads: + from cap A column 1 bottom, − from cap A column 4 bottom. Both leave through cap A's side notches and drop through the battery deck's +Y wire slot to the power deck.
  4. Put the 10 A fuse (Bussmann AGC-10 in its inline holder, TD §5.1) on the + lead, then solder it to P1 (M17, PACK IN +) and the − lead to P2 (O17, PACK IN −). Twist the pair.
  5. Before connecting: measure the pack at the two leads. About 10 to 11 V with charged cells. About 2.5 V low means one cell is in backwards. The firmware also refuses to arm below 9.6 V.
  6. With the pack connected and nothing plugged into the carrier, measure 5 V between the MPM3610's 5V pin (K9) and GND (K8). Anything else: stop and tell me.
  7. Battery or bench supply into P1 and P2, never both at once. To charge, take off cap B (three M2 screws) and slide the cells out.

Before you power it

  1. The drawing shows the XIAO from above with USB-C at the top edge, so 5V, GND and 3V3 are the top three pins on the right: holes B12, C12 and D12. Before soldering, confirm those three pins on the XIAO you have been using on the breadboard.
  2. Diode D1: the stripe (cathode) goes in B15, at the top, toward the 5 V row. Capacitor C1: the stripe (minus) goes on the GND bus.
  3. With nothing plugged in, beep 5V to GND, 3V3 to GND, and PACK + to GND. None of them may beep. Then beep each row of the Connections table.
  4. Pads that must stay clean, because they sit between two different nets: D15 (3V3 trace and Hall OUT), G15 (D8 trace and J3 GND), and the gaps between the parallel traces in rows B to G on the right of the XIAO. On the power deck, L8 on the GND bus sits next to the 5V wire in L9. Look along every trace with a light behind the board before power.
  5. Build order: jumper a first (it runs under the XIAO), then the XIAO and the low parts, then the other jumpers, then the solder traces, then the wires leaving the board.
  6. The GND wire between the boards is required even without the buck. Without it the battery reading floats, like the 0.756 V we chased earlier.
  7. First power: USB only, no bench supply. Then tell me, and I'll reboot the box and check every sensor in its log before anything spins.
