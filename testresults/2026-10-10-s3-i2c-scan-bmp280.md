# Test: BMP280 dropout after a replug, I²C address scan, and rewire on the XIAO ESP32-S3

- Date/time and timezone: 2026-10-10, 18:04 to 18:24 (America/New_York).
- Operators and witnesses: Julian (replugged the board at 18:02:40 and rewired the BMP280 twice); Julian's Claude Code agent (scanner, flash, serial capture, analysis).
- Julian's explicit go for any motor-turning step: Not applicable. No ESC or motor was connected.
- Component model, markings, and serial/lot: XIAO ESP32-S3 (MAC e0:72:a1:fc:49:5c); Adafruit MPU-6050 (#3886), QMC5883P (#6388) and BMP280 (#2651) on STEMMA QT cables.
- Firmware branch/commit and ESP32 target:
  - Firmware probes: the R3.4 builds at 873b5e3 (bench) and the diagnostic build (ELF 8f07bf892…).
  - Scanner: a throwaway ESP-IDF v6.0 app built outside the repo, [source saved here](2026-10-10-s3-i2c-scan-bmp280/i2cscan-main.c). It uses the new `i2c_master` driver on port 0 with SDA GPIO5 and SCL GPIO6, internal pull-ups on, and `i2c_master_probe` on 0x08 to 0x77 with a 50 ms timeout, over 5 passes 1 s apart. It shares no code with the firmware's i2cdev drivers.
- Procedure and acceptance-criterion reference: none planned. This was fault-finding after R3.2's boot probe (BMP280 chip ID 0x58 at 0x77) started failing.
- Rig setup, wiring revision, guard, and stop method: the breadboard rig. The A1104 was added on D10 at the same replug as the first rewire (before the scan). Nothing can move.
- Power source, set voltage, current limit, and measured voltage: laptop USB 5 V. Not measured.
- Instruments, calibration or positive-control check: the scan's positive control is the other two boards on the same bus. If they answer and the BMP280 does not, the scanner and the bus are working.

## Outcome

- Overall: **Pass after rewiring.** The fault was in the BMP280's wiring, not the firmware or the bus: two independent probes agreed, and the other two boards answered throughout. What exactly was wrong in the wiring was not recorded.
- Faults, unexpected motion, heat, noise, or supply current limiting:
  - The breadboard connections are fragile: a replug was enough to drop one board off the bus.
  - The firmware's "chip ID 0x08" message on a failed read is misleading. It should say the read failed rather than print a value.
