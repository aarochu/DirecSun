# Test: I²C address scan on the XIAO ESP32-C3 bench controller (MPU-6050, QMC5883P, BMP280)

- Date/time and timezone: 2026-10-10 (America/New_York). Per-step times were not recorded; serial timestamps below are ms since boot.
- Operators and witnesses: Aaron (wiring, power measurements, reconnects). A partner offered the power-supply hypothesis recorded below. No other witnesses recorded.
- Julian's explicit go for any motor-turning step: Not applicable; sensor bus only, no motor or ESC connected.
- Component model, markings, and serial/lot: Board silkscreen "Seeed Studio XIAO-ESP32-C3"; ESP32-C3 chip rev v0.4. Sensors per the earlier log: Adafruit MPU-6050 (#3886), QMC5883P (#6388), BMP280 (#2651), chained with STEMMA QT cables. Early in the session the MPU-6050 was assumed to be a GY-521-style board; that was not verified.
- Firmware branch/commit and ESP32 target: ESP32-C3 bench firmware in `~/esp/DirecSun` (not a git repository, so no commit). Built with ESP-IDF v6.1.0 (a standalone install at `~/esp/esp-idf-6.1`; the first build used PlatformIO's copy). The scanner uses the legacy `driver/i2c.h` driver. It probes 0x08–0x77 with a write-address transaction at 100 kHz, internal pull-ups enabled, 50 ms per-address timeout, SDA = D4/GPIO6, SCL = D5/GPIO7. A debug routine that also tries every ordered pair of GPIO 2–10 was added midway through (10 ms timeout); see the observations on its hits. Both are temporary bring-up code.
- Procedure and acceptance-criterion reference: None. This was an exploratory bring-up, not a planned T-step, and it does not count as T-7, T-8, T-9 or the 400 kHz bus check (TD A-23).
- Rig setup, wiring revision, guard, and stop method: Breadboard, XIAO powered over USB-C. External 2 kΩ pull-ups on SDA and SCL to 3V3 (operator-stated; some photos were unclear about their placement), plus the breakouts' own pull-ups when connected. Blue (SDA) wire was first found one pin off and moved to D4 (see step 2). Exact wiring revision was not recorded.
- Power source, set voltage, current limit, and measured voltage: USB 5 V into the XIAO; sensors on the XIAO 3V3 pin. The operator reported about 3.3 V between VCC and GND at the MPU-6050. No other voltage was measured, and no SDA/SCL idle voltage, continuity or pull-up resistance reading was taken.
- Instruments, calibration or positive-control check: Handheld multimeter (model and calibration not recorded). Positive control: the BMP280 and QMC5883P were each detected on the same wires and code that missed the MPU-6050.

The all-pins scan reported many hits in several runs (8 with a single device, 40 and 184 on the stuck bus). They were not investigated and are not counted as evidence. On the single-device runs, only the SDA = GPIO6, SCL = GPIO7 hit is a real connection.

## Outcome

- Overall: **Partial.** Each sensor was detected alone at its expected address (MPU-6050 0x68, QMC5883P 0x2C, BMP280 0x77), and all three were detected together at 100 kHz in the QMC5883P + MPU-6050 chain with the BMP280 wired separately. The full three-board chain MPU-6050 ↔ QMC5883P ↔ BMP280 failed and was not re-tested. The 400 kHz bus check was not run.
- Faults: (a) The first MPU-6050 attempt found nothing; it later worked when reconnected, and the cause was not identified. (b) The full chain gave a stuck bus (all probes timed out). No cause was confirmed. Hypotheses, none tested: a bad QT cable or port on the QMC5883P–BMP280 link, a bad contact, or too many pull-ups in parallel. A partner suggested insufficient power; the logged evidence is a hung bus with no resets or brownouts observed, but the serial log was not checked for resets and no supply current was measured, so this is not ruled out.
- Raw data and photos: Serial transcripts were not saved to files; the scan lines above were read from the live serial port (`/dev/cu.usbmodem101`, which re-enumerated as `/dev/cu.usbmodem1101` after the reconnects). Two photos of the breadboard were shared in chat only and are not saved here.
- Follow-up owner and action: Aaron. (1) Re-test the full MPU-6050 ↔ QMC5883P ↔ BMP280 chain with a different QT cable and with the BMP280 at the other end. (2) Measure SDA/SCL idle voltage and remove the extra 2 kΩ pull-ups to see whether the result changes. (3) Watch the serial log for resets with all three connected. (4) Save the scanner transcript and commit the scan code. (5) Remove the all-pins debug scan. (6) Repeat the combined scan at 400 kHz and read back sensor data in a separate session log.
