# R2.1: Printed flywheel with sealed washers and Hall magnets

**Status:** planned
**Files:** `cad/flywheel.py` (new), `cad/flywheel_hub_test.py` (new, the TD plate 8 thread or fit test), `cad/stack.py` (shared wheel and magnet dimensions), `cad/assembly.py` (real wheel instead of the stand-in, new positive control), `cad/out/*`, `cad/README.md` (print list, pause instructions)

## Spec

**Problem:** The motor's M6 thread is only 6.76 mm long (S-15). That leaves room for one nut and about 3.5 mm of washers, so the clamped washer stack of TD §4.5 cannot work. The owner chose a printed PETG wheel with the washers sealed inside (2026-10-10); `washer_stack_test.py` rehearses that pocket and its pause. The Hall magnets are too large to glue to the 28 mm bell, so the wheel must carry them too.

**Approach:**
- **Body.** A PETG disc at 100% infill, printed flat. It contains the `washer_stack_test` pocket, with the same stepped ribbed post, crush ribs, `PAUSE_Z`, and seven washers (37.88 mm OD). The pocket and post dimensions are imported from shared constants moved into `stack.py`, so the test and the wheel cannot drift apart.
- **Magnets.** Two pockets, diametrically opposite, at the largest radius the guard ring allows (TD §4.7: 76 mm guard ID, with the wheel keeping at least 5 mm radial clearance). They are sized from the measured magnets plus `HOLE_COMP`, and sealed in at the same print pause, with the south face toward the Hall sensor.
- **Hub interface.** Chosen after the motor-top measurement (`_set.md` question 2):
  - **A, bell screws:** a printed flange bolts to the bell's screw pattern, and the shaft passes through a clearance bore that centres the wheel.
  - **B, shaft thread:** a printed boss threads onto the M6 shaft and is locked by one nut plus thread-locker.

  Either way, centring comes from a printed feature that fits the shaft or bell to within the `HOLE_COMP`-corrected tolerance. Imbalance is the main dynamic risk (TD §3.5).
- **Balance.** The body is symmetric. Any one-sided feature (the hub flat, the pause marker) is mirrored or counter-weighted in the CAD. The geometric centre of mass is reported by `flywheel.py`, and must be within 0.05 mm of the axis.
- **`flywheel_hub_test.py`.** A 10 mm tall ring with just the hub interface, printed first to check the fit on the real motor. This is TD print plate 8.
- **`assembly.py`.** The stand-in wheel is replaced by `flywheel.py`'s solid. The clearance to the guard ring and to the Hall post is reported. A new positive control, a wheel 6 mm larger in diameter, must collide with the guard ring.

## Tests
- [ ] `tools/build-cad.sh` passes: every part is watertight, including `flywheel` and `flywheel_hub_test`.
- [ ] `assembly.py`: no overlaps, and all positive controls fire, including the new oversize-wheel control. The guard clearance (at least 5 mm) and the Hall-face gap are printed and recorded here.
- [ ] `flywheel.py` reports the centre-of-mass offset from the axis under 0.05 mm, along with its mass and I_z, which feed R2.3.
