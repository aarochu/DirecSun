# R2.3: Safety analysis for a printed rotating part

**Status:** planned
**Files:** `docs/sizing.py`, `docs/sizing_output.txt`, `docs/technical-design.md` (§2 constraints, §3.2 to §3.5, §4.5 to §4.7, §8.3, §10.1, §12.1, §12.4), `docs/firmware-design.md` (§12.2, §16, §23 rows, only if any limit changes), `changes/CONTEXT.md` (the rejected-alternatives line)

## Spec

**Problem:** The TD's safety case rests on a rule this design breaks: "nothing printed may spin with the wheel" (§2 constraints, §4.5, invariant §8.3). Its speeds are also computed for three 2.5 in washers: a 2400 rpm bias, a 4000 rpm cap with 5.7 J stored, and a 4100 rpm trip. The real wheel is a printed PETG body around seven 37.88 mm washers plus two magnets, with a different I_w. The firmware's cap is a compile-time ceiling of 4000 rpm (FD §21 rule 2), and it changes only through a TD revision like this one.

**Approach:**
- **`sizing.py`** gains a "printed wheel" case. It takes the mass and I_z of the PETG body from `cad/flywheel.py`, plus the seven measured washers and the two magnets. It recomputes everything Section 3 reports: I_w, rate per rpm, absorbable spin, slew, energy at cap and at trip, rim speed, and imbalance forces.
- **New calculation: hoop stress in the printed body** at the overspeed trip and at the sensing-lag worst case (TD §3.5). It uses the rotating-disc formula with PETG density, and adds the centrifugal load of the washers and magnets bearing on their pockets. The margin is stated against a layer-adhesion strength labelled [A]; printed parts are weakest across layers. The pass criterion is a safety factor of at least 4 at the worst-case speed. If that fails, the cap comes down. The firmware cap may be lowered, but may not be raised above 4000 rpm.
- **Bias and cap.** These are chosen again from the new numbers, using the same reasoning as TD §3.4 and §3.5: the bias sits midway in the usable range, and the cap is the lower of the PR-4 energy limit and the stress limit. A lower cap means `omega_cap_rpm` and the trip change in the FD parameter table and in `kBypassThrottleMax`'s rationale (Set 3). A higher cap is not allowed.
- **Rewrite in the TD:**
  - §2: the constraint becomes "no printed part carries rim load except the flywheel body, whose stress margin is stated in §3.5".
  - §4.5: the printed wheel, with the pause procedure from `cad/README.md`.
  - Invariant §8.3 becomes "the flywheel is the only printed rotating part, and `sizing.py` states its stress margin at the trip speed".
  - §10.1: new hazard rows for wheel burst, magnet ejection, and a washer working loose in its pocket, each with a mitigation (the guard ring, the cap, inspection) and a detection method.
  - §12.4: one log row for each change.
- **Guard-ring containment.** The guard ring (§4.7) must still contain the worst fragment. Compute the energy of one washer released at the trip speed, compare it with the energy of the TD's original 2.5 in washer, and state whether the 2.4 mm PETG guard is still adequate [A], or needs to be thicker.
