# R3.3: Hall wheel speed and ADC inputs

**Status:** in progress
**Files:** `firmware/components/direcsun_core/{include/direcsun/core/wheel_speed.hpp,src/wheel_speed.cpp}`, `firmware/test/host/test_wheel_speed.cpp`, `firmware/components/direcsun_drivers/{include/direcsun/drivers/{hall,adc_inputs}.hpp,src/{hall,adc_inputs}.cpp}`, `firmware/test_apps/hall_adc/` (its own IDF project)

## Spec

**Problem:** Overspeed protection, the wheel loop, and spin-up completion all depend on wheel speed from the A1104 Hall switch (FD §10.4). The undervoltage interlock depends on the battery ADC (FD §10.5). Neither exists yet. The Hall magnets cannot be mounted on the motor until Set 2 solves the attachment, but the sensor itself can sit on the breadboard and be tested with a magnet held in the hand.

**Approach:**
- **Core `wheel_speed`** (pure, FD §10.4 and §11.2). The ISR side supplies a ring of edge timestamps, and the core turns them into:
  - `speed_fast`: from the latest period, Ω = 30 / p rpm (two edges per turn).
  - `speed_safe`: the median of the last 4 periods.
  - A plausibility window from 3 ms to 200 ms; periods outside it are discarded and counted as glitches.
  - The staleness bound: if no edge has arrived for more than twice the last period, the estimate is replaced by 30 / elapsed.

  The API takes timestamps in µs and "now", so it needs no clock.
- **Driver `hall`.** GPIO9 (D10), falling edge, internal pull-up plus the external 12 kΩ. An IRAM ISR stores `esp_timer_get_time()` into a ring of 8 `int64_t` and publishes an atomic write index (FD §7.4). Only the control task reads it.
- **Driver `adc_inputs`.** `adc_oneshot` on ADC1 at 12 dB attenuation, with the curve-fitting calibration scheme.
  - Battery: GPIO8, channel 7. V = mV / 0.1803, plus a 1 s moving average for the interlock.
  - Sun A and B: GPIO1 and GPIO2, channels 0 and 1, read raw. Calibration belongs to Set 5.
  - ADC2 is never used, because it does not work with Wi-Fi on.
- **`firmware/test_apps/hall_adc/`** prints each edge period, both speed estimates, and the glitch count. Every 200 ms it prints vbat (instantaneous and averaged) and the sun A/B millivolts.

## Tests
- [x] HT-2 host test passes:
  - 30 / p conversion at 800, 2400, 4000, and 4100 rpm (FD §6 table).
  - The plausibility window rejects 2.9 ms and 201 ms and accepts 3 ms and 200 ms.
  - The median rejects a single bad period.
  - The staleness bound falls as elapsed time grows.
  - A positive control: a period sequence that crosses 4100 rpm is reported above 4100 by `speed_safe`.
- [x] Hall on the breadboard, with a magnet held in the hand: each pass of the south pole adds exactly one edge, and the north pole adds none (A1104 is unipolar, TD §4.6). Marked BLOCKED, not passed, if the A1104 is not wired yet.
- [ ] Hall speed from real edges: a spinning magnet gives a plausible rpm. Split from the item above on 2026-10-10 and deferred by Julian to the first flywheel spin (the printed wheel with its two magnets, on the R3.8 bench, with Julian's go), because a magnet turned by hand cannot pass the sensor faster than once per 200 ms.
- [x] Battery ADC against the multimeter, two independent instruments. With the bench supply feeding the divider at 10.0 V, vbat reads within 2% of the meter. At 9.0 V it follows within 2%. A broken channel would sit still while the supply moved.
- [ ] Sun ADC: covering and uncovering each OP550 moves its channel by more than 10x the noise. Marked BLOCKED if the OP550s are not wired yet; they are only needed in Set 5.

- 2026-10-10: Hall on the breadboard, XIAO S3, `hall_adc` app at 872f624. Output read with a raw serial reader that stamps host arrival times.
  - Wiring (Julian): A1104 VCC from the XIAO's 5 V pin, OUT to D10, with GPIO9's internal pull-up on. His meter read 5 V from VCC to GND and 3.27 V at OUT with no magnet. (The meter showed both as negative because the leads were reversed.)
  - First tries: about 10 passes gave 3 edges, then a run of holds gave 0. Cause, found by Julian with the meter on OUT: only one face of the magnet switches the A1104, as a unipolar south-pole switch should, and most early passes used the other face.
  - Handling noise: while wires and probes were being moved, D10 saw bursts of edges (14 in 3 s, with gaps of 2.4 to 14 ms). The A1104's hysteresis cannot produce that, so it is contact bounce. It stopped once nothing touched the board.
  - Clean counted run (19:03, nothing touching the board):
    - 5 south holds: 5 edges, one per hold, about 4 s apart.
    - 10 south passes about 1.2 s apart: 9 edges. One 2.46 s gap, twice the rhythm, is a pass that never switched the sensor. The ISR count equals the accepted count, with 0 lost, so the board neither missed nor doubled an edge.
    - 5 north holds and 10 north passes: 0 edges.
  - D9 (battery input, no divider fitted yet):
    - In the early runs, the floating ADC reading on D9 dropped to about 0 V during some Hall lows.
    - Julian's voltmeter read about 0 V on D9 throughout, with and without the magnet. A voltmeter on a floating pin reads 0, while a pin wired to the 3.27 V output would read 3.27 V at idle. So D9 has no wired path to the Hall output, at most a weak stray coupling, which the divider's ~18 kΩ source should swamp.
    - The battery item rechecks this with the meter.
  - Reading the status line: `glitch_count()` counts restarts after a gap over 200 ms as well as sub-3 ms rejects, as the FD specifies. In a hand test the "glitches" figure is therefore mostly restarts. The `glitch:` log lines are the true rejects: 1 all session, during handling.
  - Spin by hand (19:08): 4 edges in 10 s, with gaps of 1.29 s, 0.33 s, and 4.38 s, all outside the 3 to 200 ms window, so no rpm. A magnet turned by hand does not pass the sensor fast enough or close enough.
