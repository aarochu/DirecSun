# R3.3: Hall wheel speed and ADC inputs

**Status:** in progress
**Files:** `firmware/components/direcsun_core/{include/direcsun/core/wheel_speed.hpp,src/wheel_speed.cpp}`, `firmware/test/host/test_wheel_speed.cpp`, `firmware/components/direcsun_drivers/{include/direcsun/drivers/{hall,adc_inputs}.hpp,src/{hall,adc_inputs}.cpp}`, `firmware/test_apps/hall_adc/` (its own IDF project)

## Spec

**Problem:** Overspeed protection, the wheel loop, and spin-up completion all depend on wheel speed from the A1104 Hall switch (FD §10.4). The undervoltage interlock depends on the battery ADC (FD §10.5). Neither exists yet. The Hall magnets cannot be mounted on the motor until Set 2 solves the attachment, but the sensor itself can sit on the breadboard and be tested with a magnet held in the hand.

**Approach:**
- **Core `wheel_speed`** (pure, FD §10.4 and §11.2). The ISR side supplies a ring of edge timestamps, and the core turns them into:
  - `speed_fast`: from the latest period, Ω = 30 / p rpm (two edges per turn).
  - `speed_safe`: the median of the last 4 periods.
  - A plausibility window from 3 ms to 200 ms; periods outside it are discarded and counted as glitches.
  - The staleness bound: if no edge has arrived for more than twice the last period, the estimate is replaced by 30 / elapsed.

  The API takes timestamps in µs and "now", so it needs no clock.
- **Driver `hall`.** GPIO9 (D10), falling edge, internal pull-up plus the external 12 kΩ. An IRAM ISR stores `esp_timer_get_time()` into a ring of 8 `int64_t` and publishes an atomic write index (FD §7.4). Only the control task reads it.
- **Driver `adc_inputs`.** `adc_oneshot` on ADC1 at 12 dB attenuation, with the curve-fitting calibration scheme.
  - Battery: GPIO8, channel 7. V = mV / 0.1803, plus a 1 s moving average for the interlock.
  - Sun A and B: GPIO1 and GPIO2, channels 0 and 1, read raw. Calibration belongs to Set 5.
  - ADC2 is never used, because it does not work with Wi-Fi on.
- **`firmware/test_apps/hall_adc/`** prints each edge period, both speed estimates, and the glitch count. Every 200 ms it prints vbat (instantaneous and averaged) and the sun A/B millivolts.

## Tests
- [x] HT-2 host test passes:
  - 30 / p conversion at 800, 2400, 4000, and 4100 rpm (FD §6 table).
  - The plausibility window rejects 2.9 ms and 201 ms and accepts 3 ms and 200 ms.
  - The median rejects a single bad period.
  - The staleness bound falls as elapsed time grows.
  - A positive control: a period sequence that crosses 4100 rpm is reported above 4100 by `speed_safe`.
- [ ] Hall on the breadboard, with a magnet held in the hand: each pass of the south pole adds exactly one edge, and the north pole adds none (A1104 is unipolar, TD §4.6). Spinning the magnet by hand gives a plausible rpm. Marked BLOCKED, not passed, if the A1104 is not wired yet.
- [ ] Battery ADC against the multimeter, two independent instruments. With the bench supply feeding the divider at 10.0 V, vbat reads within 2% of the meter. At 9.0 V it follows within 2%. A broken channel would sit still while the supply moved.
- [ ] Sun ADC: covering and uncovering each OP550 moves its channel by more than 10x the noise. Marked BLOCKED if the OP550s are not wired yet; they are only needed in Set 5.
